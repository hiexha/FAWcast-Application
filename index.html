    Farmer guide

    What to do about Fall Armyworm

    Report it early.If you see Fall Armyworm in your corn, tell your Municipal Agriculture Office (MAO) right away. They can check your field and give traps, natural enemies, or advice on spraying.

    1. What to do at each risk level

    Find your province's risk on the Forecast, Map or Rankings page, then follow these steps.

    2. How to spot Fall Armyworm

    Eggs

    Small round eggs in clusters of 100 to 200, often covered with fuzzy hairs. Look under the leaves. They hatch in 2 to 3 days.

    Worm (larva)

    A light-colored upside-down Y on the head, and 4 dark dots in a square near the tail. Grows up to about 4 cm.

    Damage

    Papery "windows" and ragged holes on young leaves, and wet sawdust-like droppings inside the whorl. Worms also bore into ears.

    Moth (adult)

    Brownish-gray front wings and pale back wings. Flies at night and can travel far, which is why outbreaks spread between provinces.

    3. How to check your field

    1. Walk across your field in a zigzag (W) shape and stop at 5 places.
    2. At each stop, look closely at 10 corn plants: the leaves, the whorl and the underside of leaves. That is 50 plants in total.
    3. Count the plants with fresh damage, eggs or worms. Write the number down with the date.
    4. Check young corn most carefully. Fall Armyworm attacks every stage, but young plants are hurt the most.
    5. If the number of damaged plants keeps going up, or many plants have damage, tell your MAO.

    4. Ways to control it

    Keep the field clean

    • Remove weeds in and around the field.
    • Plow and prepare the soil well. This kills the pupae in the soil.
    • Plant at the same time as your neighbors.
    • Rotate crops or plant other crops in between corn.

    By hand

    • Crush egg clusters you find under leaves.
    • Pick off and kill worms in the whorl.
    • Pheromone traps catch moths and show when they arrive. Ask your MAO for traps and lures.

    Natural enemies

    • Earwigs eat the eggs and young worms.
    • Trichogramma are tiny wasps that destroy the eggs.
    • Metarhizium is a fungus that kills the worms.
    • These are available from the DA Regional Crop Protection Center through your MAO.

    Spraying (only when needed)

    • Try botanical (plant-based) sprays first.
    • Use chemical sprays only when damage is high. Ask your MAO which one to use.
    • Use only approved products, and follow the label.
    • Spray into the whorl, where the worms hide.
    • Wear gloves, a mask and long sleeves. Keep children away.

    5. Who to contact

    • Municipal Agriculture Office (MAO): your first stop for reports, traps, natural enemies and advice.
    • Provincial Agriculture Office (PAO)
    • DA Regional Crop Protection Center
    • Bureau of Plant Industry (BPI)

    Based on the Department of Agriculture's Fall Armyworm leaflet (DA Caraga, “Pagmatikod ug Pagdumala sa Fall Armyworm”) and DA–BPI control measures. Always follow your local agriculture office's advice.

    How FAWcast works

    The model

    FAWcast is a hierarchical logistic regression trained on . It adds up ten inputs into a score z, then turns z into a probability. Each region and province also gets its own baseline adjustment.

    How well it predicts

    Use with care

    • Quiet reporting can hide real infestations.
    • Sudden outbreaks in provinces with no recent reports are hard to forecast a quarter ahead. Run the monthly update after the first month.
    • This is a decision-support baseline. Confirm risk with field scouting.
    P(outbreak) = 1 / (1 + e−z),   z = β0 + β1x1 + … + β10x10 + region effect + province effect
